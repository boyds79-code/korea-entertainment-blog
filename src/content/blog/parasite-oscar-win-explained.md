At the 2020 Academy Awards, Bong Joon-ho's *Parasite* did something no film had ever done before: it won **Best Picture** while being almost entirely in Korean. For an industry that had spent nearly a century treating "foreign film" as its own separate, lesser category, that single win rewrote the rules of what could compete for the top prize.

## What Parasite actually won

*Parasite* didn't just sneak in with one surprise award — it swept four of the night's biggest categories.

| Award | Category |
|---|---|
| Best Picture | Top award of the night |
| Best Director | Bong Joon-ho |
| Best Original Screenplay | Bong Joon-ho & Han Jin-won |
| Best International Feature Film | Representing South Korea |

Winning Best Picture and Best International Feature Film in the same year was itself unprecedented — the two categories had rarely, if ever, overlapped in Oscar history.

## Why this was such a big deal

Before 2020, no film not primarily in English had ever won Best Picture in the Academy's 92-year history. Subtitled films were routinely funneled into the "Best Foreign Language Film" category (renamed Best International Feature Film in 2019) and rarely crossed over into the top race.

> 💡 Context: Bong Joon-ho had previously joked at a press conference that Americans could get past subtitles by overcoming "the one-inch-tall barrier" — a line that was widely quoted after the film's Best Picture win.

*Parasite's* win signaled that a global streaming and festival audience had already been watching subtitled film and television for years, and the Academy's voting body — which had recently expanded to include thousands of new international members — was starting to reflect that.

## What changed afterward

The ripple effects showed up quickly across the industry.

- **Streaming demand for Korean film and TV grew sharply** in the following years, setting the stage for other Korean titles to break through internationally.
- **Studios and awards campaigns paid closer attention to non-English submissions**, no longer treating them as a separate lane from the main awards conversation.
- ***Parasite* became a reference point** other prestige non-English films were compared to when discussing awards viability.

## The bigger picture

*Parasite's* Best Picture win wasn't a one-off fluke — it was the most visible moment in a longer rise of Korean film and television reaching international audiences, a trend that continued with other Korean titles finding global success on streaming platforms in the years that followed.

For international viewers who only heard about the film through award-season headlines, the takeaway is simple: a subtitled film from South Korea beat every English-language contender at the biggest stage in film, and the industry has been adjusting to that fact ever since.
