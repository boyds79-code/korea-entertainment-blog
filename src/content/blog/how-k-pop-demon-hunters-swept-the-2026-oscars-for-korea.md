On March 15, 2026, Korean cinema reached a new milestone at Hollywood's biggest night. *K-Pop Demon Hunters*, an animated feature blending supernatural action with the global phenomenon of Korean pop music, took home **two Academy Awards** at the 98th Oscars: **Best Animated Feature** and **Best Original Song** for "Golden." The wins marked a historic moment for Korean animation on the international stage and proved that Korea's creative industries could dominate beyond live-action film and music alone.

## The Double Win That Made History

*K-Pop Demon Hunters* became only the second Korean film project to win an Oscar in any category, following *Parasite*'s groundbreaking Best Picture win in 2020. But this victory was unprecedented in its own right—no Korean animated film had ever won Best Animated Feature, and the simultaneous song win underscored the film's fusion of visual storytelling and music.

The film competed against major studio releases in the Best Animated Feature category, ultimately claiming the prize in a year that saw strong competition from established Western animation houses. "Golden," the film's standout musical number, defeated other nominees in the Best Original Song category with its soaring vocals and Korean-language lyrics that resonated with Academy voters.

Director **Maggie Kang** accepted the Best Animated Feature award with a simple but powerful statement: **"This is for Korea and Koreans everywhere."** The line was met with a standing ovation and quickly circulated across social media as a defining moment of the ceremony.

## What *K-Pop Demon Hunters* Is About

For international audiences unfamiliar with the film, *K-Pop Demon Hunters* follows a fictional K-pop idol group that discovers they have supernatural abilities and must battle demons threatening Seoul—all while maintaining their grueling performance schedules and public personas. The film blends high-energy musical sequences with action-packed supernatural battles, creating a genre hybrid that appealed to both animation fans and K-pop enthusiasts.

The movie's animation style drew praise for its fluidity during concert sequences and its innovative use of color during battle scenes. Critics noted that the film didn't shy away from exploring the intense pressures of idol culture while delivering an entertaining, visually stunning narrative.

### The Song That Won It All

"Golden" serves as the film's emotional centerpiece—a power ballad performed during a pivotal moment when the protagonists must choose between their careers and saving their city. Sung primarily in Korean with English phrases woven throughout, the song showcased the kind of multilingual appeal that has made K-pop a global force.

## Why These Wins Mattered for Korean Entertainment

The Academy Awards have historically been dominated by American studios when it comes to animation, with occasional wins from Japanese filmmakers (Studio Ghibli's *Spirited Away* in 2003 remains a landmark). Korea's animation industry, while robust domestically and active in international co-productions, had never secured this level of recognition at the Oscars.

The 2026 wins arrived at a moment when Korean creative exports were already experiencing unprecedented global visibility:

- K-dramas dominated streaming platforms worldwide
- K-pop acts regularly topped international charts
- Korean films continued to earn critical acclaim at major festivals

*K-Pop Demon Hunters* proved that Korean creators could excel in animation—a medium where Korea had often worked behind the scenes on projects led by other countries—with a distinctly Korean story told on their own terms.

## The International Campaign and Road to the Oscars

The film's path to Oscar night involved a strategic international release and awards campaign that began in late 2025. Distributed globally through a major streaming platform alongside theatrical releases in key markets, *K-Pop Demon Hunters* built momentum through festival screenings and word-of-mouth among animation enthusiasts.

> 💡 Context: For a film to qualify for the Best Animated Feature category, it must have a running time of at least 40 minutes and a significant number of major characters animated, among other Academy requirements.

The dual nomination for both Animated Feature and Original Song gave the film two separate opportunities to shine during the ceremony, and both categories delivered. The Best Original Song performance during the broadcast—featuring live vocals and a specially choreographed stage presentation—introduced millions of viewers to the film's music.

## What Comes Next

Following the Oscar wins, industry observers noted increased interest in Korean animation projects from international distributors and streaming platforms. The success of *K-Pop Demon Hunters* demonstrated that animated films with Korean themes and Korean-language music could achieve both critical recognition and commercial viability on a global scale.

The wins also sparked conversations about representation and diversity in the animation industry, with many pointing to the importance of stories rooted in specific cultural contexts finding universal audiences. For Korean creators, the recognition validated years of work building an animation industry capable of competing at the highest levels.

The 2026 Oscars will be remembered as the night Korean animation arrived on the world stage—not as an outsider or novelty, but as a creative force that earned Hollywood's highest honors on its own merits. With two golden statuettes now in hand, *K-Pop Demon Hunters* proved that Korea's entertainment exports extend far beyond what many international audiences had previously imagined.
